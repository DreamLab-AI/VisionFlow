# DreamLab AI on VisionFlow: 18-month business roadmap (Nov 2026 – Apr 2028)

**Decision record:** [ADR-2021](../adr/ADR-2021-closed-loop-agent-operations-and-18-month-roadmap.md) (proposed).
**Owner:** jjohare. **Drafted:** 2026-10-30, by a four-agent Opus mesh (capability, market, operating model,
risk) and synthesised by the lead session. **Re-review:** at the end of every quarter, against the exit
tests below.

Status words follow the estate contract ([`architecture/adr-status-contract.md`](../architecture/adr-status-contract.md)):
*live* means runtime evidence exists, *staged* means built but not running in production, and ADR numbers
are repo-local, so every one is qualified with its repo. Commercial specifics (prices, named clients,
pipeline, contract terms) are deliberately absent from this public document.

---

## 1. Summary

DreamLab sells training, residentials, co-creation and an advisory retainer today. The sovereign stack
underneath VisionFlow (agentbox, VisionClaw, solid-pod-rs, the Nostr forum, visionGraph, ontomark,
dream-engine) appears on the website only as technology. This roadmap turns it into a service line,
**a governed agent fleet that DreamLab runs on the client's own hardware, under the client's keys**, and in
parallel turns DreamLab's own estate into the first fleet it runs.

The model is a deliberate adaptation of the operating style described by the founder of Spawn, a ten-person
games company (shared with the owner on 2026-10-30; every Spawn figure below is Spawn's own claim). We take
the **closed loop**: agents woken by real signals, area ownership, decisions judged against a recorded
corpus, humans spending their time on taste, deletion and ambition. We reject **"land first, repair
later" for anything a regulated client touches**: clinical, identity, cryptographic, secret, data-egress and
payment actions stay behind hard human gates, as VisionFlow ADR-2011 already requires.

Three facts shape everything else:

1. **The loop is open today.** Signals exist, but humans find them in periodic sweeps. On 2026-10-30 the
   worst signal-to-action latency was about 114 days (§3).
2. **The core stack is AGPL-3.0-only** (VisionClaw, agentbox, solid-pod-rs, nostr-rust-forum, sidestr-rs;
   verified from each repo's `LICENSE` and `Cargo.toml`). It sells as **build, operate and support**, never as
   a proprietary licence. ontomark, dream-engine and the Loom code are MIT/Apache and can be productised
   freely.
3. **Model supply is a business risk.** A US government order suspended commercial access to two frontier
   models in June 2026 ([IAPP, 2026-06-15](https://iapp.org/news/a/thought-for-the-week-us-government-order-forces-commercial-suspension-of-two-fronteir-ai-models)),
   and the model currently behind the Ontology Loom is licensed non-commercially. Vendor slots,
   degraded-mode operation and a commercially licensed local model are roadmap items, not hygiene.

**Three offer bundles.**

1. **Operated sovereign agent workstation** (agentbox + VisionClaw knowledge services + solid-pod-rs).
   DreamLab installs and runs it on the client's hardware, with receipts. The public-sector health residential
   (3–5 Nov 2026) is the reference deployment.
2. **Governed ontology and knowledge engineering** (visionGraph + ontomark + VisionClaw + the forum governance
   panel). The client's domain model lives in Markdown, is reasoned over, and changes through signed decisions.
3. **Evidence-gated repository care** (dream-engine + codescape views + the diagram and review tooling). A
   nightly service that proposes changes and keeps only those the client's own evaluators prove better. This is
   the closest thing to Spawn's janitor and break-it agents, and it is permissively licensed.

**Packaging** (models, not prices): fixed-scope diagnostic → fixed deployment sprint → monthly managed-operations
retainer, with outcome-linked components only on reversible, measurable outcomes (for example, backlog cleared,
nights green). Forward-deployed engineering is the delivery mode; the known failure is permanent services cost,
so field work must flow back into the substrates as releases.

**Licence constraints that bind the offer.**
- AGPL means clients and their network users get source rights to the stack we run for them. Contracts say so.
- Billable work cannot run on non-commercial model weights. A per-model licence register with a CI gate, and a
  commercially licensed default model for client profiles, are Q1 items.
- visionGraph's CC BY sources (SLSO, the SAC-CG agent ontology) and CC0 SWEET need a third-party notice before
  the corpus is sold.

**Headline metric: signal-to-action latency (SAL)**, the time from a signal's first machine-readable occurrence
to the first commit, journal event or issue that references it, computed from `gh run list`, the events
journal, the review ledgers, estate-health snapshot commits and dream ledger rows. **New incident class:
silent-loop-stopped**, meaning any scheduled producer misses an expected cadence. Spawn treats an hour without a
ship as its top incident; our equivalent is bounded time-to-action, not volume of change.

## 5. Assurance ladder

Health work stays **non-clinical until Q6**: knowledge engineering, estate and repository care, governance
and back-office tooling. That holds the assurance burden to the first rungs and makes the clinical rungs a
decision rather than a precondition.

| Rung | Applies when | Quarter |
|---|---|---|
| UK GDPR and a DPIA per engagement ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/)) | Any personal data | Q0 for the residential; template Q1 |
| Cyber Essentials Plus ([NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cyberessentials/overview), [PPN 014](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ppn-014-cyber-essentials-scheme)) | Contracts handling personal data or OFFICIAL information | Q1 |
| DSPT, moving to CAF alignment ([DSPT](https://www.dsptoolkit.nhs.uk/)) | Any supplier handling NHS data | Q2–Q3 |
| NCSC cloud security principles ([NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/cloud/the-cloud-security-principles)) | Framework listings | Q2 |
| DTAC ([NHS England](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/digital-technology-assessment-criteria-dtac)) | A product placed with an NHS buyer | Only when a product is placed |
| ISO/IEC 27001 | Enterprise and public tenders | Start Q3, certify Q5 |
| EU AI Act ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)) | EU-facing work; high-risk duties from 2 Dec 2027 | Applicability Q4 |
| DCB0129 / DCB0160, MHRA software as a medical device ([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/digital-clinical-safety-assurance/)) | Anything whose output feeds direct care | Decision Q6 |

The estate already produces evidence for these rungs: the hash-chained execution and events journals, gate
receipts (agentbox ADR-2087), the security-invariants and deepsec workflows, the decline floor (agentbox
ADR-2144), and ADR ledgers with verification stamps. NHS England's guidance on ambient scribes, which requires
that "users review and approve any product outputs prior to further actions"
([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/guidance-on-the-use-of-ai-enabled-ambient-scribing-products-in-health-and-care-settings/)),
is the posture a regulated buyer expects, and it is why client production is propose-only.

## 7. Unit economics (structure)

| Metric | Formula | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per delivered change | (tokens × price per backend + local compute) ÷ changes that survive 7 days without revert | Per-turn usage in the agentbox execution journal; judge prices per backend (agentbox ADR-2139); local compute: **not yet measured** |
| Cost per client estate per month | Σ cost per change + fixed runtime share + review hours × loaded rate | Execution journal per estate; review hours: **a new `exec.review` record** |
| Human leverage | delivered changes ÷ review hours | git + the review record |
| Waste ratio | tokens on reverted, refused or abandoned runs ÷ all tokens | journal + gate receipts + dream-engine verdicts |
| Judge hold rate and override rate | held ÷ judged; owner reversals ÷ decided | judge log; override log |
| Revert rate | reverts within 7 days ÷ merged | git (Spawn reports ~0.9%) |
| SAL | median and p90 signal-to-action latency | §3 sources |

Levers: local models for triage and compaction, prompt caching, evidence gating that stops a run before its
tokens are spent, and the judge's hold rate, which trades review hours against reverts.

## 8. Where we deliberately differ from Spawn

- **Regulated clients.** Clinical, identity and data-egress actions are irreversible in law even when reversible
  in code, so hard gates cover far more than payments, and "land then repair" applies only to the reversible
  internal class.
- **Sovereignty.** Client fleets run on the client's hardware with locality fixed at boot, so per-change cloud
  spend is the wrong unit of scale.
- **Evidence over volume.** dream-engine's verdict classes keep a broken testbed separate from a bad change. A
  revert rate that suits a game is not acceptable for a hospital, so the target is bounded time-to-action.
- **Humans as taste, deletion and entropy, kept.** The owner's role moves from answering a queue to setting
  principles, saying no, deleting, and asking for the 10–100× change no agent proposes on its own.
