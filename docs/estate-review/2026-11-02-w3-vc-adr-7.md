# VisionClaw ADR-2134 to ADR-2173 (group vc-adr-7)

I traced all 152 headed sections of the 16 VisionClaw records in group vc-adr-7 against the source at VisionClaw `86cdd7635582`. Twelve records are operative decisions: ADR-2134, 2135, 2136, 2138, 2140, 2141, 2142, 2147, 2148, 2156, 2168 and 2173. ADR-2139 is superseded in part: ADR-2141 replaced its decisions 1, 3, 5 and 9, while its no-delta, entitlement, fail-closed and class-flag rules still stand. ADR-2137, ADR-2170 and ADR-2171 are proposed records. The 152 rows break down as follows:

- 87 hold in code.
- 20 hold in part.
- 2 are contradicted.
- 5 are not implemented, as their proposed status says.
- 5 have been overtaken by a later section or record.
- 19 cannot be checked from source. These are dated test and runtime receipts.
- 14 are context or narrative.

The most important finding is the ADR-2134 consequence that the dev-mode relay bypass is "impossible in release". That is true only of `[profile.release]` built without the `dev-auth` feature. The bypass is compiled in under `cfg(any(debug_assertions, feature = "dev-auth"))`, and the `dev-runtime` profile, which serves the live backend (ADR-2156), turns `debug-assertions` on. The two contradictions are both internal drift:

- ADR-2135's one-done-rule table still says "half the bounds diagonal". The code, and the same section's own bullet, use the inscribed sphere.
- ADR-2171 reads `implementation_status: none` and "Verification: None yet", but its decision 2 has shipped with a unit test.
